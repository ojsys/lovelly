# M2 — Matching and text chat

**Goal (PRD):** the Redis queue, the matcher worker, the websocket, screens 02, 03 and 06,
plus blocks.
**Accept:** two test devices are matched within 10 s, Next puts both back in the queue, and
blocked pairs are never matched (unit test plus a load test with 500 simulated users).

## How a match works

```
App ──ws queue.join{mode}──► Consumer ──► Redis: ticket:{user}, queue:{mode} (ZSET by join time)
                                             ▲            │
                         matcher worker loop ┘            ▼ (every 250 ms per mode)
   pick pairs (rules below) ─► Lua script claims both tickets atomically (safe with N matchers)
   ─► MatchSession row ─► match.found to user.{a} and user.{b} (Channels groups)
```

**Pairing rules**, in order:
1. Same mode only (FR-6).
2. **Hard exclusions:** never a blocked pair, in either direction (FR-7, SF-5). Never
   someone you were matched with in the last 24 h (FR-7; the paid rematch is M7). Never
   yourself or an account that isn't active.
3. **Preference for the first 30 s:** only candidates who share ≥ 1 interest **or** ≥ 1
   language. Candidates are ranked by shared interests, then shared languages, then same
   city, then longest wait. A user who has set no interests or languages counts as
   compatible with everyone, so new users aren't stuck.
4. **After 30 s:** the search widens to anyone in the mode, and `match.widened` goes to the
   client (FR-9).
5. **SF-7:** new accounts get at most 30 matches in their first 24 h. Someone skipped within
   5 s three times in a row waits 2 minutes before matching again. Both are cheap to do in the
   matcher, so they come in now rather than in M4.

The matcher compares each ticket against at most 200 candidates per tick (oldest first). The
load test checks this stays fast at 500 users. Scaling out across several matchers is safe
because the Lua claim is atomic.

## Backend

**Models:**
- `MatchSession` (PRD fields): one row per conversation. `end_reason` is `next`, `end`,
  `peer_left` (disconnect) or `moderation` (from M4).
- `Message` (PRD fields): `session` FK. Friend chats come in M5. `flagged` and
  `flag_reason` support FR-18 / SF-8. Stranger messages are deleted after 7 days by a
  Celery beat task (PRD data model). The "unless a report holds them" exception arrives with
  reports in M4.
- `Block` (PRD fields), unique per pair.
- `Interest` / `UserInterest` (PRD fields). M2 needs them for matching, and seeds a starter
  catalogue that can be edited in admin. Editing your own interests and languages is the
  Profile screen (M5).

**Websocket `/ws/`** (PRD event names):

| Event | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `queue.join {mode, filters, accept_charge}` | Verified users only (SF-1). Non-empty `filters` → `error: filters_unavailable` until M7. Re-joining replaces your ticket. |
| `queue.leave` | Removes your ticket. |
| `match.found` → | `session_id`, `peer {first_name, age, city, shared_interests, verified}`, `room_name`. `media_token` is null for text and a fake token for voice/video until M3. |
| `match.widened` → | Sent once, at 30 s. |
| `session.next` / `session.end {session_id}` | Ends the session (`next` / `end`). On `next` the sender is re-queued in the same mode. The peer gets `session.ended {reason: peer_next \| peer_left}`. |
| `chat.message {session_id, body}` | ≤ 1000 chars, rate-limited (20 per 10 s). Stored, then delivered to both sides with `id`, `sender`, `sent_at`, `flagged`. |
| `chat.seen {session_id, message_id}` | Marks the message and everything before it as seen and tells the sender ("Seen"). |
| `chat.typing {session_id}` | Relayed to the peer, not stored. |

- **Disconnects:** a dropped socket leaves the queue at once. In a session there's a 10 s
  grace period (NFR: reconnect within 10 s without losing the session) before the session
  ends `peer_left`.
- **FR-18 / SF-8 message check:** phone numbers, 10-digit account numbers, payment/OTP phrases
  and a slur list. A hit still delivers the message, with `flagged` set, so the receiver sees
  a warning, and the sender's `risk_score` goes up by 1. The slur list starts as a small
  placeholder (see questions).
- **REST:**
  - `GET match/online-count` counts websockets seen in the last 60 s (presence ZSET).
  - `POST blocks`, `DELETE blocks/{id}`, `GET sessions/{id}`.
  - `GET me/profile` is a read-only summary for Home and Matching (first name, age, city,
    interests, languages, verified). `PATCH` arrives in M5.

## App

- **Packages:** `web_socket_channel`, plus a `RealtimeClient` with automatic reconnect
  (backoff), the JWT in the query string, and a token refresh on close code 4401.
- **`MatchController`** state machine: idle → searching → matched(session) → ended. It
  rejoins the queue after Next.
- **Bottom navigation** (Match / Friends / Profile) through a shell route. Friends and
  Profile stay placeholders until M5.
- **02 Home:**
  - Greeting by time of day and first name; the avatar shows the initial.
  - The coins pill shows **0** until the wallet exists (M6, when coins can first be earned).
  - The online count comes from the API (refreshed every 15 s).
  - Video/Voice/Text segmented switch; the Start card text changes with the mode.
  - The Plus filter tiles open the `/plus` placeholder (M7).
  - **FR-5:** without camera/mic permission, Video and Voice are disabled, with a hint to
    allow them in Settings.
- **03 Matching:**
  - The mode chip and the pulsing rings animation.
  - Your interests as chips, with "+ Edit" going to Profile (placeholder until M5).
  - A live online count (FR-9; not in the design, see questions).
  - "Widening your search…" after 30 s.
  - **Cancel only:** the design's "Match found →" button is a prototype link. The app moves
    on by itself when `match.found` arrives.
- **06 Chat:**
  - Header with leave, the initial, "Name, age" and typing… / online. The banner reads "You
    matched on X and Y." plus "Never share bank details or OTPs." (FR-18).
  - Bubbles with Sent / Seen status, quick-reply chips, a composer, and an inline warning on
    flagged messages.
  - Leaving ends the session. When the other person leaves, a "<name> left the chat" line
    appears with "Find someone new" and "Home".
- **Voice / video in M2:** matching works for all three modes. A voice or video match opens
  a minimal "Connected to <name>" screen with **Next** and **End**, so FR-12 can be tested
  now. M3 replaces it with screens 04 and 05.

## Tests and acceptance

- **Unit tests (matcher service):**
  - same-mode only
  - blocked pairs never pair, in either direction
  - no re-pair within 24 h
  - interest/language preference and the 30 s widening
  - SF-7 limits
  - the atomic claim under two concurrent matchers
  - each Next / End / disconnect path
  - message flagging
- **Load test** (`backend/loadtest/`, asyncio websocket clients run in Docker): 500
  verified users, with about 10 % of pairs blocking each other at random. Clients join,
  chat, press Next and rejoin for 3 minutes. The test **fails if any blocked pair is ever
  matched** and reports the time-to-match median and p95 (target median < 10 s).
- **Two devices:** an e2e test opens two real app `RealtimeClient`s against the live stack
  and asserts the match comes within 10 s, Next puts both back in the queue, and chat
  messages and Seen go both ways. A two-phone run is up to you, like M1.
- Widget tests and golden images for 02, 03 and 06, compared against the designs.

## Decisions to confirm

1. **Who goes back to the queue on Next.** In calls, the person who pressed Next is
   re-queued by the server, and the other person's app re-joins by itself. Together that is
   "Next puts both back in the queue". In text chat, the person left behind sees "<name>
   left the chat" and chooses "Find someone new", instead of being thrown into a new chat
   mid-read.
2. **The 30 s preference window** works as described above, so the median stays under 10 s
   while interests matter.
3. **There's no way to block in the app until M4.** The design's only block action is on the
   Report screen (08). M2 builds blocks in the backend and API and tests them in the matcher.
4. **Chat call buttons for strangers** (voice/video icons in 06, and the "Let's switch to
   voice" quick reply). The PRD only covers voice → video upgrades during a call (FR-14);
   switching from text to a call isn't specified. Until you decide, the icons are hidden in
   stranger chats. Friends' direct calls come in M5.
5. **Quick replies.** "Add as friend" is dropped until friend requests exist (M5). The other
   two send text; the middle one uses a shared interest when there is one.
6. **The gift button** in the chat composer is hidden until M6.

## Outcome (done 2026-10-20)

**Acceptance**
- **Two devices matched within 10 s; Next puts both back in the queue.**
  `app/test/e2e/match_e2e_test.dart` onboards two fresh users through the live API, then
  runs both apps' real `MatchController` + websocket client. They matched in 1.3 s; after
  Next in video mode both are searching again. A second test covers text chat with Seen and
  typing in both directions, and the "left the chat" state.
- **Blocked pairs are never matched.**
  - Unit tests cover both block directions, including after the 30 s widening and past the
    24 h window.
  - The load test (`python manage.py loadtest`): 500 users, about 12,300 blocked pairs, 3
    minutes against the production-style server (`api-load`: uvicorn, 4 workers). Result:
    **1,576 sessions, 0 blocked-pair matches, median wait 4.98 s, p95 20.6 s.**
- The p95 comes from users who share nothing until the 30 s widening.

**Bugs the load test found (fixed):**
1. One stale ticket (a deleted or suspended user) made a tick throw, which stopped matching
   for everyone. Ineligible tickets are now dropped, and a failure only affects its own pair.
2. Matcher ticks took 5.8 s on average (up to 25 s):
   - Every event opened a new event loop and Redis connections.
   - About 8 queries ran per pair.
   - Fix: events are batched per tick, sessions are created in bulk, peer summaries take a
     fixed number of queries, and only blocks between two queued users are loaded. The worst
     cold burst (247 pairs at once) went from 16 s to 2.6 s.
3. Websocket handshakes took 20–30 s under load, because every database call opened a new
   Postgres connection (`CONN_MAX_AGE` was 0). Connections now persist (60 s, with health
   checks), and the median handshake at 500 clients is 0.2 s.
4. DEBUG was forced on in any process using the dev settings; added `korram.settings.prod`.

**Not clean, and why:**
- **Worker restarts during load runs.** uvicorn workers were still restarted once or twice
  per run (each drops that worker's sockets).
  - Cause: event-loop stalls of up to 26 s, from the host Mac swapping heavily (18.5 GB of
    19.5 GB swap in use).
  - The app reconnects and the server keeps sessions for 10 s, but the load-test clients
    don't reconnect.
  - **Re-run the load test on a machine that isn't swapping before trusting the p95.**
- **No phone run.** The UI was checked with widget tests and golden images against designs
  02, 03 and 06, not on a phone.

**Small additions beyond the plan:**
- `MatchSession.ended_by` and an end reason `blocked`.
- A `queue.joined` / `queue.left` acknowledgement event.
- `KIcons` renders the design's own SVG line icons.
