# M3 — Voice and video

**Goal (PRD):** LiveKit rooms and tokens, screens 04 and 05, the 5-second blur, the both-agree
photo reveal, and the voice-to-video upgrade.
**Accept:** a call connects on 4G and on simulated 3G, mute and camera-off work, and a dropped
connection rejoins within 10 s.

## How a call works

```
match.found {room_name, media_token} ─► app joins LiveKit room (livekit_client)
                                           │  audio/video flow through the SFU (FR-10)
Django ──(LiveKit server API)──► room     │  never over our websocket
  • token: identity = user id, room = session room, short TTL, publish rights by mode
  • upgrade.accept → grant camera publish to both participants
  • session end (Next / End / disconnect / block) → delete room (kicks both)
```

## Backend

- **Real `MediaProvider`:** the `livekit-api` package. LiveKit is self-hosted in Compose
  already, so this provider can be real now; the fake stays for unit tests. Tokens grant
  join, subscribe, and publish of only the sources the mode allows (voice: microphone; video:
  microphone and camera). Rooms are deleted when a session ends.
- **Events** (PRD names, plus one addition):

| Event | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `reveal.request {session_id}` | Voice only (FR-13). Recorded in Redis and relayed to the peer. When both have asked, the server sends `reveal.accept` to both (from M5, it carries each other's main photo as a signed URL). |
| `reveal.accept {session_id}` | Client → server: agree to the peer's request (same effect as requesting). |
| `upgrade.request {session_id}` | Voice → video (FR-14); relayed to the peer. |
| `upgrade.accept {session_id}` | Grants camera publishing to both, sets the session's `upgraded_at`, and sends `upgrade.accept` to both. |
| `upgrade.decline {session_id}` | **New, not in the PRD list:** the peer said no, so the requester's "Waiting…" state can end. |

- **`POST sessions/{id}/media-token`:** **new, not in the PRD.** It issues a fresh join token
  for an active session, so a rejoin after a drop works even after the first token expired.
  Only participants of an open session get one.
- `match.found`'s peer summary gains `shared_languages` (for the "EN · Pidgin" chip).

## App

- **Packages:** `livekit_client`, wrapped in a `CallEngine` interface (join, leave, mute,
  camera on/off, speaker, remote video/audio-level streams) with a fake for widget tests.
- **Network settings:**
  - Adaptive stream, dynacast and simulcast (the low layers carry 3G, NFR: "video falls back
    automatically").
  - Audio uses the speech preset (24 kbps; NFR: voice works at 32 kbps).
  - LiveKit's reconnect (ICE restart / resume); if that fails, a fresh token and rejoin.
    Target: back within 10 s.
- **04 Video call:**
  - Full-screen remote video with top and bottom scrims, and your own preview at 104×148.
  - Name, age and verified tick; city · call timer; "Both like X" and shared-language chips.
  - Controls: mute, camera off, end (danger), and the "Next person →" bar.
  - **FR-11:** the remote video is blurred for its first 5 seconds on each side, and again
    for 5 s after a voice → video upgrade.
- **05 Voice call:**
  - "Voice · mm:ss" chip; the peer's photo, blurred, in the ring; "Name, age"; "City · likes
    X, Y".
  - Waveform bars driven by the peer's live audio level.
  - Icebreaker card with Shuffle.
  - Reveal button: "Reveal photos (both must agree)", then "Waiting for <name> to reveal too…".
  - Controls: mute, speaker, switch to video (FR-14; the other side gets an Accept/Decline
    sheet), Next, end.
- **Report button** (top right on both screens) opens the `/report/:sessionId` placeholder
  until M4 builds screen 08.
- **FR-5:** a video call, or accepting an upgrade, checks camera and mic permission first and
  offers Settings if blocked.
- The interim "Connected" screen from M2 is deleted.

## Tests and acceptance

- **Backend unit tests:**
  - Token grants per mode, and that a voice token can't publish camera.
  - The upgrade grant.
  - Reveal needs both sides, and the event order doesn't matter.
  - The room is deleted on every session-end path.
  - Media-token refresh is limited to participants of open sessions.
- **SFU-level acceptance** (`backend/calltest/`): two headless LiveKit participants (Python
  `livekit` rtc SDK) in a Compose container join with our tokens and exchange synthetic audio
  and video through our LiveKit:
  - **4G / 3G:** one participant's container runs `tc netem` with typical 4G, then 3G
    (about 750 kbps down / 250 kbps up, 150 ms delay, 1 % loss). The call must connect, and
    audio must keep flowing on 3G.
  - **Mute / camera-off:** muting stops the audio track and camera-off unpublishes video, as
    the other side sees it.
  - **Drop and rejoin:** 100 % packet loss for 5 s, then restore. The participant must be
    back in the room, receiving, within 10 s.
- **App:** widget tests with the fake `CallEngine` (controls, blur timing, reveal states,
  upgrade sheet, Next/End, reconnect banner), and golden images of 04 and 05 against the
  designs.
- **On phones (you):** two real phones on 4G. In development, LiveKit must advertise your
  Mac's LAN IP instead of 127.0.0.1 (README will show how). On mobile networks behind carrier
  NAT a TURN server is needed; LiveKit has one built in, and it's configured for staging, not
  local dev.

## Decisions to confirm

1. **Hidden until their milestone:** Add friend (M5) and Gift (M6) on the video controls.
   The design's in-call chat bubble ("Wait, you were at Felabration too?") is dropped,
   because no screen lets you type during a call.
2. **Photo reveal before photos exist.** Profile photos arrive in M5. M3 builds the full
   both-agree mechanism; until then a "revealed" voice call shows the initial avatar unblurred.
3. **Icebreakers:** a fixed list in the app (the design's question plus about 30 more; send
   yours if you have them). Both people see the same question, chosen from the session id.
   Shuffle changes only your own card.
4. **The two additions above:** `upgrade.decline` and `POST sessions/{id}/media-token`.
5. **App size.** WebRTC adds about 10–15 MB per CPU architecture. To stay under the 40 MB
   install target (NFR), Android ships as an App Bundle (per-ABI splits), which Play does
   automatically.

## Outcome (done 2026-10-20)

**Acceptance (SFU level, `make test-calls`)**
- Setup: two headless participants with real tokens from our backend, through our LiveKit,
  with links shaped by `tc netem`. 3G here means 750 kbit, 75 ± 20 ms per packet, 1 % loss.
- **Call connects and flows on 4G and on 3G:** joined in 1.3–2.6 s; about 100 audio frames/s
  and 13–15 video frames/s each way on both profiles.
- **Mute and camera-off:** the other side sees the mute event, and video stops after camera
  off.
- **Dropped connection:**
  - 5 s blackout: audio back both ways in 0.5–2.5 s and still connected 12 s later, in every
    run.
  - 15 s blackout: LiveKit's own resume works in most runs. The harness also rejoins with a
    fresh token when LiveKit gives up, like the app does.
  - 4 of 5 consecutive runs passed. The failing run's details weren't captured, and the
    Mac was swapping heavily at the time (see M2). Re-run on a quiet machine.
- **FR-14:** a voice-call token cannot publish camera.

**App**
- Screens 04 and 05 are built against the designs (golden images in
  `app/test/goldens/video_call.png` and `voice_call.png`).
- Widget tests cover:
  - joining with the match token
  - the 5 s blur (and again after an upgrade)
  - mute and camera off
  - Next and End, and the other person pressing Next
  - "Reconnecting…" and a rejoin with a fresh token
  - both-agree reveal, in both directions
  - voice → video request, accept and decline
  - FR-5 declining video without camera permission
  - speaker
- **Not tested here: the Flutter LiveKit engine on a real device.** It's behind the
  `CallEngine` interface and covered only through the fake. Calls between two phones are
  the remaining manual check (README: "Calls on a real phone").

**Changes made while building**
- **API client moved to its own package** (`app/packages/korram_api`). The code generator
  (retrofit_generator) needs protobuf 5, and livekit_client needs protobuf 6, so they can't
  share one dependency resolution.
- **Version pins:**
  - `livekit_client` 2.6.4: the newest Flutter 3.32 allows (2.6.5+ needs meta 1.17); 2.4.8
    did not compile against current protobuf.
  - Android NDK 27.0.12077973, required by the WebRTC plugins.
  - Android Gradle Plugin 8.7.3 → 8.9.1: androidx.core 1.18, pulled in by the WebRTC
    plugins, requires it.
  - Debug APK builds with LiveKit and WebRTC.

**App size (NFR: install under 40 MB)**
- Release APK per CPU type: **arm64 31.7 MB**, armeabi-v7a 24.5 MB (x86_64 36.6 MB, for
  emulators).
- Under the target, but WebRTC uses most of the budget, leaving about 8 MB for M4–M7. Keep
  an eye on it, and ship as an App Bundle.
- **Speaker default:** voice calls start on the earpiece and video on the speaker. The design
  shows the speaker button off.
- **LiveKit's advertised media IP** is configurable (`LIVEKIT_NODE_IP`) for testing on a
  phone.
